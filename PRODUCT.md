# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Couples who want a lightweight way to check in on their emotional life together. One or both partners open the app on their own phone, in short private moments during the day (a spare minute at work, before bed), to log how they're feeling. The job to be done on any given screen is narrow and quick: pick a type, pick an emotion, write a sentence or two, optionally attach a photo, save, done. Nothing here is a multi-step workflow — every screen should support a 10-30 second interaction, not a sit-down session.

## Product Purpose

Together lets each partner privately journal short emotional entries (worries, joys, gratitude, wishes, thoughts) through the week. Entries stay private until an AI-generated weekly report surfaces a shared narrative connecting both partners' weeks — never raw entries out of context, always the woven story. A calendar view lets each partner browse their own entries and see when (not what) their partner has been journaling, with content unlocking only once a report has processed that period. Success looks like: two people who each write a little consistently, and come away from the weekly report understanding each other a bit better than a text-message summary of the week ever could.

## Brand Personality

Warm, soft, unhurried. The tone is a gentle friend, not a clinical instrument and not a corporate tool. Cream/sand backgrounds, sage and rose accents, a serif display face (Fraunces) paired with a rounded sans (Quicksand) for warmth without cuteness overload. Copy is calm and non-judgmental ("Расскажите, что вы чувствуете" rather than "Log your mood"). Nothing here should ever feel like homework.

## Anti-references

- Cold, clinical mood-trackers (chart-heavy, graph-first, quantified-self aesthetics)
- Corporate SaaS dashboards / enterprise B2B tools
- Anything that turns journaling into a chore via friction, guilt copy, or streak-shaming mechanics

## Design Principles

- Fast in, fast out — the entry screen is a 10-30 second interaction, not a form to fill out carefully
- Text and photo are the point; every picker/chrome element around them is secondary and should recede
- Privacy is structural, not just stated — locked/read-only states and "hidden until report" states must be visually unambiguous, never look like a bug
- Warmth over efficiency-signaling — soft shapes, generous but not empty whitespace, no sharp corporate edges
- One clear next action per screen; never make the user guess what happens if they tap something

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard mobile accessibility: WCAG AA contrast (4.5:1 body text, 3:1 large text/icons), minimum 44x44pt tap targets, respects system font scaling and reduced-motion settings. No specialized accommodation requirements beyond that.
